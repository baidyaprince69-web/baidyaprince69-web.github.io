The person behind the strings

A familiar melody,
a different voice.

I'm Prince Baidya, the violinist and creator behind Violin Prince. This is an independent music project built around a simple idea: a familiar song can reveal a different side of itself when its melody is carried by the violin.

My listening world moves across Bengali songs, Rabindra Sangeet, Hindi film and indie melodies, English-language favourites and music from different traditions. Each brings its own character. A lyrical Bengali melody, a much-loved film song and a contemporary pop ballad do not ask for the same phrasing or atmosphere, and that variety is part of what makes the instrument so interesting to explore.

I think of a cover as an interpretation, not an attempt to replace the original. The melody people recognise remains at the centre, while the violin gives it another voice through tone, articulation, dynamics and expressive phrasing. The goal is to keep the song's identity while allowing the instrumental performance to feel personal.

The work also continues after the performance is recorded. A clear violin tone, a balanced mix and a thoughtful sense of space help the listener stay close to the melody. Production choices should support the music rather than distract from it.

This website is the official artist home: a place to discover the project, read about its musical direction, find selected performances and follow the official profiles. The creative idea behind each cover

Keep the melody.
Let the violin speak.

A song is more than a sequence of notes. Its identity lives in the way a phrase rises, settles, pauses and returns. The Violin Prince approach is to start by listening closely, then find an instrumental voice that respects the original while making room for a new interpretation.

01 / LISTEN ♫

Understand the song first

Before thinking about technique or effects, pay attention to the melody that makes the song memorable: its mood, movement, rhythm and emotional centre. That is the thread the violin performance needs to carry.

02 / INTERPRET 𝄞

Give the melody a violin voice

On violin, expression comes through more than pitch alone. Bow direction, articulation, dynamics, vibrato and the shape of a phrase can change how a familiar line feels while keeping it recognisable.

03 / PRESENT ◈

Let the sound serve the music

Recording and production help bring the performance to the listener. Tone, balance, ambience and supporting textures matter most when they make the violin clear, expressive and comfortable to hear.

Different musical traditions deserve their own character. The intention is not to make every song sound alike, but to let each melody lead the interpretation.

Beyond the performance

The craft behind the sound.

A violin recording is shaped by more than the notes. The instrument's natural tone, the performance dynamics, microphone perspective, room character and final balance all influence how a melody reaches the listener.

The Violin Production Playbook is a practical guide created by Prince Baidya for violinists and home-recording musicians. It organises recording and processing ideas by purpose, from clean capture and corrective tone shaping to expressive colour, ambience and mix placement.

The guiding principle is restraint with intention: begin with a performance that communicates clearly, then use production to support its character. A warm tone does not need to become muddy, and a spacious sound should not push the melody away. The most useful setting is the one that serves the song.
